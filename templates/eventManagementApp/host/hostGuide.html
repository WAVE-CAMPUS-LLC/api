Setting Up Your Event

Event Basics

The basics lay the foundation for your event. Here's what you need to do:
  • Event Title
    • Choose a descriptive and engaging title for your event.
    • Keep it clear and concise. (minimum 5 characters). Example: "Spring Tech Fest 2024"
    • Avoid overly generic titles like "Party" or "Conference".
  • Event Type
    • Select a category that best fits your event. Options include Academic, Music, Social, Technology, Workshop, and more.
    • The event type helps potential attendees understand what to expect and improves discoverability.
  • Event Description
    • Provide a detailed yet engaging description of your event.
    • Highlight the purpose, what attendees can expect, and why they should attend.
    • Use bullet points or short paragraphs for readability.
  • Location
    • Add the venue details, including the building name, street address, and city.
    • For virtual events, provide a link or platform name (e.g., Zoom, Microsoft Teams).
    • Ensure the location is accurate to avoid confusion for attendees.

Date and Time

Timing is everything when planning an event. Here's how to set it up:
  • Event Date
    • Use the date picker to select the event date. Ensure it aligns with your target audience's availability.
    • For recurring events, create separate events for each date.
  • Start and End Time
    • Select the event's start and end times.
    • Add buffer time before and after for setup and attendee arrival.
    Tips for choosing the right time:
    • Avoid scheduling during major holidays, exams, or other local events that may conflict.
    • For evening events, consider attendees' commutes and other commitments.

Event Status

The status helps manage your event throughout its lifecycle. Here's what each status means:
  • Incomplete
    • The default status when you start creating an event.
    • Events with this status are not visible to attendees. Use this as a draft mode while you work on the details.
  • Active
    • Indicates the event is ready and visible to potential attendees.
    • Ensure all essential details (title, type, date, etc.) are finalized before marking an event as active.
  • Live
    • The event is currently happening.
    • Use this status to let attendees know they can still join if applicable.
  • Archived
    • For events that have ended or are no longer relevant.
    • Archived events remain in your dashboard for record-keeping but are hidden from the public.

Privacy and Discoverability

Event Privacy

Controlling who can view and attend your event is crucial for its success. In this section, you'll learn how to configure privacy settings and set discoverability options to align with your event goals.
  • Privacy Settings

    The privacy setting determines the visibility of your event. Choose an option that best fits the purpose of your event

    • Discoverable
      • What it means: The event will appear on the WAVE explore page, making it accessible to a broader audience.
      • Who can see it: Anyone using the platform.
      • When to use it: Use for public events where you want to maximize attendance. Examples include workshops, fundraisers, and social gatherings.
    • Invite-Only
      • What it means: The event is private, and only invited guests can view or attend.
      • Who can see it: Only users with an invitation.
      • When to use it: Use for exclusive gatherings like VIP dinners, private parties, or team meetings.
    • Private (College)
      • What it means: Only users from your college can view and attend the event.
      • Who can see it: Verified members of your college on WAVE.
      • When to use it: Use for college-specific events like club meetings, study groups, or campus fundraisers.
    • Private (Organization)
      • What it means: Only users from your specific organization within a college can access the event.
      • Who can see it: Verified organization members.
      • When to use it: Use for organization-specific events such as Greek life socials, departmental workshops, or team meetings.

Discoverability Levels

For events marked as Discoverable, you can further refine who can see and attend.
  • Open to Anyone
    • The event is open for anyone to view and attend without restrictions.
    • Use this for events aimed at the general public or large audiences.
  • RSVP Required
    • Users must RSVP to attend.
    • Great for tracking attendees or limiting participation to a manageable number.
  • Restricted by College or Organization
    • The event is visible on the explore page but only accessible to users within your college or organization.
    • Ideal for semi-private events where you want broader visibility but attendance limited to a specific group.
    Open to Anyone

    • Engage with restrictions: For restricted events, make sure the right groups are aware through targeted communication.
    • Use RSVP to gauge interest: Collect RSVP data to anticipate attendance numbers and prepare accordingly.
    • For invite-only events: Ensure you send invitations well in advance, with clear instructions for accessing the event.

Location and Contact Details

Providing clear and accurate location and contact information is essential to ensure attendees can easily find and reach out about your event. This section will guide you through filling out the necessary details for your venue and contact methods.

Venue Information

The venue sets the tone for your event. Here's how to provide complete and useful information:
  • Building Name
    • Enter the name of the building where the event will take place (e.g., “Student Union Hall” or “Tech Center Auditorium”).
    • This helps attendees identify the venue easily, especially on large campuses or in busy areas.
  • Street Address
    • Provide the full address, including the street name and number.
    • Example: "123 University Way."
  • Apartment, Suite, or Unit (Optional)
    • If the venue is part of a larger building, provide additional location details such as room numbers, suites, or floors.
    • Example: "Room 305, East Wing."
  • City, State, ZIP Code
    • Ensure all fields are complete so attendees can use GPS or mapping apps to find the location accurately.
  • Campus Display (Optional)
    • If applicable, you can choose to display the event on your college’s page for better visibility among your campus community.

Contact Information

Make it easy for attendees to get in touch with the event organizers. Fill out the following details:
  • Contact Email
    • Provide an email address where attendees can send questions or requests related to the event.
    • Example: events@college.edu.
  • Contact Phone Number
    • Include a phone number that attendees can call or text for urgent inquiries.
    • Example: (555) 123-4567.
  • Contact Website (Optional)
    • Add a website link for additional event details, registration, or RSVP.
    • Example: www.eventname.com.
  • Social Media Links (Optional)
    • Include handles or links to the event's social media pages, such as Instagram.
    • Example: @waveoncamp for Instagram.
    • Use these channels to promote the event and engage with your audience.

Ticketing Options

Ticket Types

Define how attendees will access your event. Choose the ticketing option that best suits your event's needs
  • Paid/Free Tickets
    • Paid Tickets: Set a price for tickets if your event requires payment for entry. Use this option for fundraising or premium events.
    • Free Tickets: Provide free access but still require ticket registration for better attendee tracking.
  • RSVP Required
    • Use this option when you want attendees to confirm their participation without purchasing a ticket.
    • Great for workshops, networking events, or small gatherings.
    • Attendees will be asked to provide their name and contact information upon registration.
  • Invite Required
    • Restrict access to attendees who receive a personal invitation.
    • Ideal for exclusive events, private parties, or invite-only fundraisers.
    • Only individuals with an invite can register or purchase a ticket.
  • Mixed Ticketing Options (Optional)
    • Combine multiple ticket types for complex events.
    Tips for Ticket Types
    • Consider your event goals when choosing a ticket type.
    • Clearly communicate ticketing requirements in your event description.

Guest-list

The guest list provides a way to manage and display attendees for your event. Here's how it works:
  • Managing the Guest List
    • RSVP and Ticket Tracking: All registered attendees, regardless of ticket type, are automatically added to the guest list.
    • Use this list to confirm attendance or check in guests at the event.
  • Display Options
    • Public Guest List: Let attendees opt to display their names on the event’s guest list.
    • Private Guest List: Keep the list visible only to the event host and administrators.
  • Guest List Filters
    • Filter the list by ticket type, RSVP status, or other criteria to easily manage attendee details.
    Export Options
    • Export the guest list as a CSV file to use it for event check-ins or follow-ups.
Visuals and Media

Photos

Photos play a crucial role in showcasing your event's essence. This subsection enables you to upload and manage images that visually represent your event.
  • Purpose of Adding Photos
    • Set the Tone: Use images that reflect your event's theme, vibe, and audience. For example, a professional seminar may use images of conference halls, while a music festival may showcase vibrant, lively scenes.

Promoting Your Event

College Visibility

Promoting your event within a college ensures that it reaches students, staff, and other members of the college community. This is especially effective for events designed for a specific college audience or hosted within a college.
  • Steps to Promote
    • Feature on College Pages: Use the option to display your event on your college’s page if applicable.
    • Email Campaigns: Collaborate with the college administration to include your event in newsletters or email blasts.
    • Campus Posters: Design posters and place them on campus notice boards, student centers, or popular areas.
    • Student Groups and Clubs: Partner with college organizations to co-promote your event to their members.

Organization Visibility

If your event is tied to a specific organization (e.g., a student club, professional network, or community group), promoting it within that organization’s network is crucial.
  • Steps to Promote
    • Organization Pages: Display your event on your organization's page on WAVE or similar platforms.
    • Direct Invitations: Use the organization's contact list to send personalized event invitations.
    • Internal Communication: Share details through organization-specific channels like Slack, Discord, or internal newsletters.
    • Collaboration: Engage other organizations on campus or locally to co-host or co-promote the event.

Social Media and Websites

Social media and websites offer the widest reach, enabling you to promote your event beyond local or college-specific boundaries.
  • Social Media Promotion
    • Platforms to Use: Focus on platforms where your audience is most active, such as Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, or TikTok.
    • Event Hashtags: Create a unique hashtag for your event to encourage sharing and discussions.
    • Countdown Posts: Share engaging content leading up to the event, such as sneak peeks or "10 days to go" graphics.
    • Collaborate with Influencers: Partner with social media influencers or community leaders to amplify your reach.
    • Stories and Reels: Use Instagram or Facebook Stories and short-form videos to capture attention.
  • Website Promotion
    • Dedicated Event Page: If you have a website, create a page specifically for your event with detailed information and registration links.
    • Blog Posts: Write articles about the event's purpose, speakers, or agenda to generate interest.
    • SEO Optimization: Use relevant keywords so that people searching for related events can find yours.
Best Practices

Plan Ahead

Early planning is essential for a successful event. Taking time to set goals, secure resources, and finalize logistics minimizes last-minute stress and ensures everything runs smoothly.
  • Key Steps:
    • Define Objectives: Clearly outline what you want to achieve, whether it’s raising funds, building awareness, or fostering connections.
    • Set a Timeline: Use a detailed timeline with deadlines for tasks like venue booking, ticket sales, and promotions.
    • Secure Vendors: If your event requires catering, equipment, or entertainment, book these services early to avoid availability issues.
    • Budget Wisely: Account for all costs, including unforeseen expenses, to stay financially prepared.

Engage your Audience

A memorable event keeps attendees engaged from start to finish. Providing interactive experiences and personal touches ensures participants feel involved and valued.
  • Key Strategies:
    • Interactive Content: Incorporate live polls, Q&A sessions, or games to keep the audience engaged.
    • Personalization: Use attendee data (e.g., preferences or interests) to tailor the event experience. For instance, greet participants by name or recommend sessions based on their profile.
    • Event Hosts and Moderators: Assign dynamic hosts to keep the energy high and interactions smooth.
    • Networking Opportunities: Include designated times for attendees to connect with one another, such as breakout sessions or networking lounges.

Follow Up

After the event, maintaining communication with attendees keeps your brand in their minds and lays the foundation for future engagement.
  • Key Steps
    • Send Thank You Notes: Email or message attendees to express appreciation for their participation.
    • Share Event Highlights: Post photos, videos, or testimonials from the event on your platform or social media.
    • Feedback Surveys: Ask participants for feedback on their experience to identify what worked well and areas for improvement.
    • Provide Resources: Share additional content like presentation slides, recordings, or a summary of the event.

Data Analytics

Collecting and analyzing data from your event helps you measure its success and refine your strategy for future events.
  • Key Steps
    • Attendance: Compare registration numbers to actual attendance.
    • Engagement: Monitor activities like poll participation, Q&A submissions, or social media mentions during the event.
    • Revenue: If applicable, evaluate ticket sales and other revenue streams.
    • Feedback Trends: Analyze feedback forms to identify recurring themes or issues.
  • Tools and Techniques
    • Event Platform Analytics: Use built-in analytics tools (e.g., WAVE's dashboard) to track performance.
    • Surveys and Forms: Use platforms like Google Forms or Typeform to gather attendee feedback.
FAQs

Commonly asked questions

The FAQs section is designed to address common questions your audience may have about the event creation process, platform features, and troubleshooting. This section saves time by providing quick answers to frequently encountered issues.
  • General Questions
    • Q: What types of events can I create on WAVE?
      A: You can create a variety of events, including social gatherings, fundraisers, workshops, networking events, and more.

    • Q: Do I need to pay to host an event on WAVE?
      A: Hosting an event on WAVE is free. However, certain features like advanced analytics or promotional tools may come with additional costs.

    • Q: Can I edit my event after it goes live?
      A: Yes, you can edit certain aspects of your event, such as the description or ticketing options, even after it goes live.
  • Setting Up Your Event
    • Q: Can I save my event progress and continue later?
      A: Absolutely! WAVE allows you to save your progress at every step of the event creation process so you can return and continue at any time.

    • Q: How do I set up an event that repeats regularly?
      A: You can use the recurring event option during the "Date and Time" setup to specify daily, weekly, or custom schedules.

    • Q: What if I don't have a venue confirmed yet?
      A: You can select "TBD" under the venue section and update the information later once the venue is finalized.
  • Privacy and Discoverability
    • Q: What is the difference between a discoverable and an invite-only event?
      A: Discoverable events are visible to the public or specific groups (e.g., colleges or organizations), while invite-only events require attendees to have an invitation to view or participate.

    • Q: Can I restrict who sees my event within a college or organization?
      A: Yes, you can set privacy restrictions to limit visibility to specific groups, like a department or club within your college or organization.
  • Ticketing Options
    • Q: Do I need to offer tickets for my event?
      A: No, tickets are optional. You can create free events without requiring tickets or set up ticketing options if necessary.

    • Q: Can I offer different ticket types?
      A: Yes, you can create multiple ticket tiers, such as general admission, VIP, or early-bird tickets, with custom pricing and availability.

    • Q: How does the guest list work?
      A: The guest list shows attendees who opted to display their names. You can enable or disable this feature when creating the event.
  • Promoting Your Event
    • Q: How do I make my event visible to my college or organization?
      A: During event setup, select the option for college or organization visibility in the "Promoting Your Event" section.

    • Q: Can I share my event on social media?
      A: Yes, WAVE provides direct sharing options for popular platforms like Facebook, Twitter, and LinkedIn. You can also copy a unique event link to share elsewhere.

    • Q: Does WAVE help with event promotion?
      A: Yes, WAVE offers built-in tools like featured listings and email campaigns to help promote your event to a wider audience.
  • Troubleshooting and Support
    • Q: I can't save my event. What should I do?
      A: Check if all required fields are filled out. If the issue persists, try refreshing your browser or contact our support team.

    • Q: What if I accidentally delete my event?
      A: Deleted events cannot be restored, but you can create a new event with the same details.

    • Q: How do I contact support?
      A: You can reach our support team via email or the live chat feature on the WAVE platform.
